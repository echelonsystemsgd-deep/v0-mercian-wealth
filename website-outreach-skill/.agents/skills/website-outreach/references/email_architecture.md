# Master Email Architecture & Sequence Doctrine (AI Knowledge Base)

This reference teaches the AI the exact pedagogical doctrines, mental models, block-by-block structures, and decision logic for constructing cold email sequences from raw variables.

Students must learn to write these emails through their coursework. The AI uses this architecture to generate, evaluate, validate, and dynamically construct outreach without relying on static templates.

---

## 1. Outreach #1: First Contact Architecture

### Objective
Outreach #1 has one job: **earn a reply that keeps control of the conversation**.
It never attempts to explain an offer, justify the agency, or push for a call. Those trigger avoidance. Instead, it creates three sensations:
1. *This is about me.*
2. *This reduces risk.*
3. *This has a clear next step.*

### The 9 Golden Rules of Outreach #1
1. **Relevance before identity**: Never lead with who you are. Lead with the observable reason this email exists for them.
2. **One lane only**: Confined strictly to the operational lane tied to the customer's next step.
3. **One friction only**: One visible point of hesitation on one page/step. Never send an audit.
4. **No internal measurement claims**: Describe consequence as mechanics, not metrics (no fake analytics or bounce numbers).
5. **Truth posture**: Observed details are stated plainly. Buyer behavior is framed as patterns, not measured certainty.
6. **Risk reversal through boundaries**: Time safety (< 2 min) and evaluation safety (ignoring ends it).
7. **Micro-yes only**: A single binary question answerable in one line. Never a meeting or call request.
8. **Subject alignment**: The subject line is a label for the decision you want.
9. **Mandatory forwardability**: Must be clean enough to be forwarded internally without embarrassment.

### The 9-Block Construction Model
- **Block 1: The Opener** (Relevance proof): References the public website or operational lane. Must be unique to that specific business.
- **Block 2: The Friction & Location**: Points directly to one visible flaw on one specific page in neutral language.
- **Block 3: The Recognizable Reason**: Explains why a real buyer hesitates at that step (shared reality, never blame).
- **Block 4: Consequence as Mechanics**: Describes the natural chain reaction that occurs when that friction exists (cause-and-effect without numbers).
- **Block 5: Evaluation Artifact & Scope Boundary**: Mentions a quick inspection mockup/preview tightly scoped to that one issue.
- **Block 6: Risk Reversal**: Signals evaluation takes under two minutes.
- **Block 7: Micro-Yes**: The repeatable, low-cost ask (e.g. *"Want me to show you?"*).
- **Block 8: Closing**: Sender name only. No marketing signatures, links, or bloat.
- **Block 9: Clean Line Spacing**: Every block separated by double newlines.

## 2. Follow Up #2: Progress, Not Reminders

### Objective
Follow Up #2 is sent **+3 days** after Outreach #1 inside the original thread.
It is **not a reminder**. Phrases like *"checking in"*, *"just bumping"*, *"circling back"*, or *"did you see my email"* are **strictly banned** because they signal neediness and low status.
Follow Up #2 turns the first message from a claim into an observable pattern.

### Decision Logic & Rules
1. **Shorter than Outreach #1**: Shorter signals respect for their time.
2. **Same lane, same friction**: Never switch topics or add new issues.
3. **Restate reason as an operator pattern**: Explain why that friction matters in general operator reality.
4. **Consequence as plain mechanics**: Remind them how hesitation causes mobile visitors to abandon without taking the next step.
5. **Restate free value & repeat identical micro-yes**: Keeps the decision easy and safe.

### Block Structure
- **Block 1: Minimal Re-entry**: Contextual acknowledgment without apology or guilt.
- **Block 2: The Reason Anchor**: One sentence stating why you pointed at the friction.
- **Block 3: Consequence Mechanics**: Cause-and-effect chain in plain language without metrics.
- **Block 4: Value Line & Repeat Micro-Yes**: Restate the quick mockup offer and repeat the exact same micro-yes.
- **Block 5: Clean Closing**: Sender name only.

## 3. Follow Up #3: Scope Compression & The Final Useful Nudge

### Objective
Follow Up #3 is sent **+2 days** after Follow Up #2 inside the thread.
It reduces cognitive load and risk to the minimum. The reader has seen the lane; now they need effort reduction.

### Decision Logic & Rules
1. **Shorter than Follow Up #2**: Compressed to the smallest possible unit.
2. **One friction, one outcome only**: Narrowing, not expanding.
3. **Add a boundary**: Explicitly state you will stop following up after this if it isn't relevant. Boundaries remove sales pressure and signal professionalism.
4. **Zero guilt language**: No referencing that they didn't reply.
5. **Identical micro-yes**: Still the same low-friction decision.

### Block Structure
- **Block 1: Minimal Re-entry**: One short line to place the message in the thread.
- **Block 2: Scope Compression**: Clarifies that the preview only covers that one page/layout so mobile visitors move smoothly to the next step.
- **Block 3: Boundary Sentence**: Declares evaluation takes under two minutes, and confirms you will stop following up after this.
- **Block 4: Repeat Micro-Yes**: The same one-line decision.
- **Block 5: Closing**: Sender name only.

## 4. Follow Up #4: The Binary Ask

### Objective
Follow Up #4 is sent **+2 days** after Follow Up #3 inside the thread.
This is the final touchpoint before archiving or moving to the phone Call List.
It is **not persuasion—it is administration**. It reduces the decision to an instant binary choice (yes or no).

### Decision Logic & Rules
1. **Extremely short**: Shorter than Follow Up #3 (~15–20 words total).
2. **No new information**: No new arguments, claims, or frictions.
3. **One binary question**: Ask directly whether to send the preview across or pass.
4. **No meeting request**: Keep the decision about receiving the asset, not booking a call.
5. **Zero passive aggression**: Calm, polite, professional exit.

### Structure
- **Block 1: One-sentence Binary Question**: e.g., *"Should I send across that [page] mockup for [Company], or pass for now?"*
- **Block 2: Closing**: Sender name only.

## 5. Variable Y: The Input Standard

Variables are the facts and patterns behind every message. The AI must enforce these 10 Golden Rules:

1. **Observed or Conditional**: Point to public reality or general buyer patterns. Never state guesses as measured facts.
2. **No Internal Measurement Claims**: Mechanics over metrics. No fake conversion, bounce, or revenue numbers.
3. **One Lane Only**: Locked to the specific customer next step (e.g. Menu $\rightarrow$ Book Table).
4. **One Friction Only**: Pick the single friction closest to the customer's next step.
5. **One Consequence Only**: Direct cause-and-effect tied to that friction.
6. **One Repeatable Micro-Yes**: A one-line, binary question safe to say no to.
7. **No Opinion Variables**: Words like *"outdated"*, *"ugly"*, *"bad layout"* are banned. Describe physical mechanics.
8. **No Flattery Variables**: Praise reads like a cheap sales tactic.
9. **Names are Optional**: Only include a first name when verified with high confidence.
10. **The Pass/Fail Test**: If a variable cannot be built truthfully without guessing, skip the lead.

## 6. The "Yes Flow" Protocol (When a Lead Replies)

When a prospect replies positively (*"Sure"*, *"Send it over"*, *"Yes please"*):

### The 6 Operating Steps:
1. **Speed**: Reply quickly while the thread is warm (momentum decays fast).
2. **Deliver Cleanly**: Send a clean, view-only link on a trusted domain. **NO attachments. NO logins. NO forms.**
3. **Lower Click Fear in One Sentence**: Confirm it is view-only, takes under 2 minutes, and focuses strictly on that one workflow.
4. **Direct Attention with One Instruction**: Tell them the primary thing to look at (e.g. *"Notice how section 2 lets diners skim dishes without vertical scrolling"*).
5. **Ask Only ONE Natural Qualifying Question**: Confirms operational pain or reality without interrogating (e.g. *"Is mobile menu navigation something your team has noticed diners running into, or is the current flow working fine?"*).
6. **Bridge to a Call ONLY When Earned**: Propose a call only after they view the asset or confirm the pain. Frame it as a 10-minute scope and risk check, not a sales pitch.
