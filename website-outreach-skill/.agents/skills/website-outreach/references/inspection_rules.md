# Hard Rules: Website Visual & Commercial Inspection

When auditing a prospective client's website for redesign outreach, you must conduct a rigorous, grounded visual and commercial review. Your goal is NOT to write an academic audit or find petty cosmetic flaws. Your goal is to identify **visually verifiable problems substantial enough to support a credible, natural cold conversation about rebuilding their website**.

---

## 1. The 14 Flaws You Must Actively Inspect

Inspect both **Desktop** (1920x1080) and **Mobile** (390x844) views for these 14 flaws:

| # | Flaw | What to Look For | Commercial Angle for Cold Outreach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dated / Templated Appearance** | Clunky boxed layouts, generic builder sections, default-looking themes, stock imagery, repetitive cards, or a visibly DIY assembly. | Makes the company look interchangeable, inexpensive, or neglected compared with stronger competitors. |
| 2 | **Weak or Missing Hero** | Above-the-fold banner lacks a compelling headline, uses generic stock images, or fails to state what the business actually does. | Visitors bounce within 5 seconds without understanding what is offered. |
| 3 | **Unclear Value Proposition** | Vague slogans ("Excellence in service", "Your trusted partner") without explaining specific offerings or unique selling points. | Fails to give prospects a concrete reason to choose them. |
| 4 | **Missing or Weak CTA** | No visible primary action button above the fold, low contrast buttons, or vague wording ("Click here" vs "Book Free Consultation"). | Leads visitors into dead ends, forfeiting inquiries. |
| 5 | **Unclear Visitor Journey** | No obvious logical flow guiding the visitor from interest to education to action; page feels disconnected. | High drop-off rate as prospects get lost or frustrated. |
| 6 | **Weak Visual Hierarchy** | All text is the same weight/size; headings blend into body copy; eyes do not naturally scan to key points. | Visitors skim, miss core services, and leave without retaining anything. |
| 7 | **Poor Trust Signals & Social Proof** | Missing reviews, buried testimonials, lack of accreditations/certifications, or hidden client logos. | Kills credibility when high-ticket buyers search for reassurance. |
| 8 | **Generic Design (Lacks Brand Identity)** | Looks like a free generic template that could belong to any company in any industry; zero personality. | Makes the brand forgettable and forces them to compete solely on price. |
| 9 | **Visual Clutter & Competing Elements** | Too many banners, contradictory popups, competing buttons, or crowded walls of text without breathing room. | Overwhelms the visitor, causing decision paralysis and immediate bounces. |
| 10 | **Weak Mobile Experience** | Text overlaps on mobile, horizontal scrollbars appear, menus break, or images push core content off the screen. | Over 60% of traffic is mobile; a broken mobile layout instantly loses leads. |
| 11 | **Confusing Navigation** | Overstuffed navigation bars, non-standard naming, broken multi-level drop-downs, or missing mobile hamburger menus. | Visitors cannot find pricing, services, or contact details quickly. |
| 12 | **Buried Important Content** | Core services, pricing guides, or portfolio pieces hidden 3 clicks deep or at the very bottom of a long unformatted page. | High-intent buyers leave without discovering the company's best offerings. |
| 13 | **Mismatch: Business Quality vs Website Quality** | The business is clearly established, high-end, or experienced, but the site looks amateurish, DIY, or cheap. | The website drastically undersells the true caliber of the company. |
| 14 | **Broken UI / Rendering Issues** | Overlapping text blocks, unclickable elements, broken image links, awkward white gaps, or misaligned columns. | Damages trust immediately, signaling poor attention to detail. |

## Builder and Template Judgment

Wix, WordPress, Squarespace, and similar platforms are not flaws by themselves. Judge the visible result. When a site clearly presents as a cheap, minimally customized template, classify it decisively as **Dated / Templated Appearance**, **Generic Design**, or **Business Quality vs Website Quality** rather than excusing it because it technically works.

Strong evidence includes several of these traits working together:

- default-looking hero, typography, buttons, cards, icons, or section spacing;
- generic stock imagery and copy that could belong to almost any business;
- repeated drag-and-drop sections with weak hierarchy or no intentional visitor journey;
- inconsistent fonts, colors, image crops, alignment, or button styles;
- visible theme, widget, plugin, or builder artifacts;
- desktop sections that merely stack on mobile without adapting priorities;
- a polished or established business represented by a visibly DIY presentation.

Do not infer the CMS from appearance alone. Name Wix or WordPress only when the source, footer, asset paths, or another direct signal verifies it. The internal audit may describe the presentation as cheap, templated, generic, or DIY when the evidence supports that judgment. The outreach email must translate that judgment into one concrete, respectful observation; never insult the owner, call the site “shit,” or criticize the platform itself.

## 2. Selection Rule: Pick the Single Strongest Issue

- **Never list all problems in an outreach email.** Listing multiple issues sounds like an aggressive spam audit report.
- **Select the #1 issue** that meets all three criteria:
  1. **Immediately recognizable**: The owner or decision-maker will instantly recognize what you are referring to without needing a developer to explain it.
  2. **Directly tied to lost revenue/leads**: It is obvious how this flaw causes prospective clients to leave, hesitate, or abandon a booking.
  3. **Commercially justifies a redesign**: It cannot be dismissed as a minor 2-pixel spacing tweak.

---

## 3. Strict Grounding & Anti-Hallucination Rules

1. **Only report what is directly visible**: Never invent broken links, hidden forms, or backend performance problems you cannot see.
2. **Never use generic buzzwords**:
   - ❌ BANNED: "conversion rate optimization", "digital presence", "user engagement", "UX friction", "modern aesthetic", "synergy".
   - ✅ REQUIRED: Concrete, conversational language (e.g., *"the large central image pushes the booking button completely off-screen on phones"*).
3. **Praise genuine strengths**: If the site is already modern and sleek, acknowledge it. If a site is truly good, do not invent flaws—classify it as `FINE` and move to the next lead.
4. **Mobile normalcy**: A standard hamburger menu or normal vertical scrolling on a phone is NOT a bug. Only flag mobile behavior if elements visibly break, clip, or create severe friction.
